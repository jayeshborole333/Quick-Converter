Meter

Definition: A meter, or metre (symbol: m), is the base unit of length and distance in the International System of Units (SI). The meter is defined as the distance traveled by light in 1/299,792,458 of a second.

History/origin: Originally, in 1793, the meter was defined as one ten-millionth of the distance from the equator to the North Pole. This changed in 1889, when the International prototype metre was established. In 1960, the meter was again redefined in terms of wavelengths of a certain emission line of krypton-86.

Current use: The meter is used worldwide as the SI unit of length for measuring distance, height, width, depth and many other dimensions.

Foot

Definition: A foot (symbol: ft) is a unit of length in the imperial and US customary systems of measurement. One foot is defined as exactly 0.3048 meters. One foot contains 12 inches and one yard contains three feet.

History/origin: The foot has historically been used in many measurement systems, including Greek, Roman, English, Chinese and French systems. The exact length varied between different systems before international standardization.

Current use: Feet are commonly used in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom for everyday measurements such as height, shorter distances, field lengths, altitude and elevation.
