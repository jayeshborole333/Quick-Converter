Liter

Definition: A liter (symbol: L) is a unit of volume accepted for use with the International System of Units (SI), but not a SI unit itself. It is equal to 1 cubic decimeter (1 dm³), or 1,000 cubic centimeters (cm³).

History/origin: The liter was introduced in France in 1795 as part of the metric system and originally defined as one cubic decimeter.

Current use: The liter is used worldwide to measure liquid volumes, especially in food and beverage packaging, fuel consumption, and scientific research.

Gallon (US)

Definition: A US gallon (symbol: gal) is a unit of volume in the US customary system. It is exactly 3.785411784 liters.

History/origin: The US gallon originates from the English wine gallon used in the 1700s.

Current use: The US gallon is used mainly in the United States to measure fuel, beverages, and containers.
