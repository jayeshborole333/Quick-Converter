Liter

Definition: A liter (symbol: L) is a unit of volume accepted for use with the International System of Units (SI), but it is not an SI base unit. One liter is equal to one cubic decimeter (1 dm³), or 1,000 cubic centimeters.

History/origin: The liter was introduced in France in 1795 as part of the metric system and was originally defined as one cubic decimeter.

Current use: Liters are used worldwide to measure liquid volumes, especially for beverages, fuel, cooking, containers, scientific measurements, and everyday quantities.

Gallon (US)

Definition: A US gallon (symbol: gal) is a unit of volume in the US customary system. One US gallon is exactly equal to 3.785411784 liters.

History/origin: The US gallon has historical roots in English units of volume and became standardized as part of the United States customary measurement system.

Current use: The US gallon is primarily used in the United States for measuring fuel, beverages, liquid containers, and other volumes.
